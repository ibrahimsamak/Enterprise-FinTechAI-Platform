# Common Fraud and Scam Typologies

## 1. Purpose

This reference describes the fraud and scam patterns most often seen in
outbound payments at FinAI Bank, and the questions officers should ask to
recognize them. In a scam the customer usually makes the payment themselves,
so a confirmed payment is **not** automatically a legitimate one.

## 2. Business email compromise (BEC)

**Pattern:** a fraudster impersonates a supplier or executive by email and
asks for an urgent payment, or for payment details to be changed to a new
(usually foreign) account.

**Signals:** new payee replacing a long-standing one; urgency; payment
instructions received only by email; destination country differs from the
supplier's usual country.

**Ask the customer:** "Did you confirm the new bank details by phone using a
number you already had for this supplier?"

## 3. Romance scam

**Pattern:** a person met online builds a relationship over weeks or months,
then asks for money for travel, medical bills, customs fees or an investment.

**Signals:** repeated international payments to an individual the customer has
never met in person; increasing amounts; customer reluctant to explain.

**Ask the customer:** "Have you met this person in person? Who suggested this
payment?"

## 4. Investment and cryptocurrency scam

**Pattern:** the victim is shown fake returns on a trading platform and asked
to send larger amounts, often to overseas accounts or crypto exchanges.

**Signals:** payments to crypto exchanges or offshore brokers; promised
guaranteed or very high returns; "fees" required to withdraw profits.

## 5. Impersonation scam (bank, police, government)

**Pattern:** a caller claims to be from FinAI Bank, the police or the Canada
Revenue Agency and tells the customer their money is at risk or that they owe
money, then directs a transfer to a "safe account".

**Signals:** customer under time pressure; customer told not to tell the bank
the real reason; payment at unusual hours while the caller is on the line.

**Key fact for customers:** FinAI Bank will **never** ask a customer to move
money to a "safe account" or to share a one-time passcode.

## 6. Money mule activity

**Pattern:** the customer's account receives funds from unrelated third
parties and quickly sends them abroad, sometimes after answering a "work from
home" job advert.

**Signals:** incoming transfers from several unrelated senders followed within
hours by an international outbound payment of a similar total.

**Action:** money-mule activity is a money-laundering concern - always
escalate to Compliance (CMP-002).

## 7. Structuring

**Pattern:** splitting a large amount into several payments just below a
reporting or review threshold.

**Signals:** several payments of similar amounts just under CAD 5,000 or
CAD 10,000 within a short period.

**Action:** structuring is a money-laundering indicator - escalate to
Compliance (CMP-002), even if each individual payment scored LOW.
